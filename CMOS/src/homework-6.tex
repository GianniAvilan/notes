	\item Constant field scaling allows us represent changes in the scale of CMOS devices with a unitless quantity \(S\). Assuming ideal constant field scaling (that is, scaling the size of a CMOS device without changing the strength of its internal electric field), we may use Table 7.4 to determine that gate delay \(\tau\) scales by a factor of \(\frac{1}{S}\). Thus, using an initial gate delay of \(\tau = 500 \ \mathrm{ps}\), we may assume that by scaling the device in question by a factor of \(S=2\), the new gate delay \(\tau = \frac{500}{2} = 250 \ \mathrm{ps}\).

		Using Table 7.5, we see that repeated wire RC delay per unit length, assuming constant field scaling of gates, scales by a factor of \(\sqrt{S}\); in our case, this causes the delay to \textit{increase}, so that \(t_wr = 500 \sqrt{2} \approx 707 \ \mathrm{ps}\). This concords with our intuitive sense of wire scaling and its impact on delay; reduced height in an interconnect will lower its cross-sectional area and increase its resistance, leading to higher delay.

		In total, this leads to an overall reduction in delay from \(500 + 500 = 1000 \ \mathrm{ps}\) to \(250 + 707 = 957 \ \mathrm{ps}\), or a 4.3\% reduction in delay.

	\item 
		First, the worst-case input delay for the NOR2X1 gate, based on the TSMC databook, \(B \to Y\uparrow\) is \(45 \ \mathrm{ps}\), and best-case input delay \(A \to Y\downarrow\) is \(18 \ \mathrm{ps}\).
		We will use the formula given in the TSMC databook for adjusting delay based on derating factors
		\[
			t_{\mathrm{TPD}} = (K_{\mathrm{process}} \cdot [1:e +35 
			+ (K_{\mathrm{volt}}) \cdot \Delta V_{dd})] \cdot [1+(K_{\mathrm{temp}} \cdot \Delta T)] \cdot t_{\mathrm{typical}}
		\]
		and using the allowable voltage and temperature ranges found in the databook, \(1.62 - 1.98 \ \mathrm{V}\) and \(0 - 125 \degree \ \mathrm{C}\), respectively, we may determine worst-case and best-case delay as follows:
		\begin{enumerate}
			\item At \(V_{DD} = 1.62 \ \mathrm{V}\) and \(T = 125 \degree \ \mathrm{C},\) with a slow process speed, 
				\[
					t_{\mathrm{TPD}} = 1.293 \cdot [1 + (-0.731 \cdot -0.18)] \cdot [1 + (0.00123 \cdot 100)] \cdot t_{\mathrm{typical}} = 73.9 \ \mathrm{ps}.
				\]
			\item At \(V_{DD} = 1.98\) and \(T=0 \degree \ \mathrm{C}\), with a fast process speed,
				\[
					t_{TPD} = 0.781 \cdot [1 + (-0.511 \cdot 0.18)] \cdot [1 + (0.00123 \cdot 0)] \cdot 0.018 = 12.8 \ \mathrm{ps}.
				\]
    \end{enumerate}

		\item 
			\begin{enumerate}
				\item Process size refers to the width of the transistor's gate, and so scales by a factor of \(S\), while gate delay scales with \(\frac{1}{S}\). Taking the ratio of gate delay between the two processes in this way, 
					\[
						\tau_{\mathrm{ratio}} = \frac{0.058}{0.083} \approx 0.7,
					\]
					and so, in the \(12 \ \mathrm{nm}\) process, we may expect gate delay to be roughly \(70 \%\) of gate delay in the \(17 \ \mathrm{nm}\) process.
				\item Unrepeated RC wire delay per unit length, on the other hand, scales with \(S^2\), and so the ratio between the two processes is,
					\[
						t_{wu \ \mathrm{ratio}} = \frac{17^2}{12^2} \approx 2,
					\]
					and so we may expect unrepeated RC wire delay per unit length to be roughly twice as large in the \(12 \ \mathrm{nm}\) process as in the \(17 \ \mathrm{nm}\) process. 
			\end{enumerate}
